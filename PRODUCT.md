# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Patients and caregivers receiving WSI Hive on a USB flash drive from UHN. Many readers may be elderly, anxious, tired, or unfamiliar with computers. They need to open the guide, find the right app launcher for their computer, understand the legal and clinical warning, view the provided slide images, and know where to get help.

## Product Purpose

WSI Hive is a portable whole-slide image viewer distributed on a flash drive. The guide exists to help patients use the drive safely and confidently without installing software or needing internet access. Success means a patient can identify their computer type, open the app, follow simple viewing steps, avoid damaging or changing the drive contents, and contact UHN with the right information if something goes wrong.

## Brand Personality

Simple, respectful, credible, reassuring. The experience should feel like UHN patient education: medically trustworthy, plain-spoken, calm, and supportive without sounding casual or technical.

## Anti-references

Do not make the guide feel like a generic software manual, a marketing landing page, a dense technical README, or a developer support page. Avoid fake screenshots, decorative illustrations, complex folder diagrams, unnecessary technical detail, jargon-first language, hidden gotchas, and visual styles unrelated to UHN.

## Design Principles

1. Put the patient action first.
2. Use plain language and short steps.
3. Make safety and privacy warnings clear without alarming the reader.
4. Use real system screenshots or empty placeholders only.
5. Keep UHN identity restrained, credible, and consistent.
6. Always provide a manual fallback when launch links may be blocked.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA or better. Use large readable text, high contrast, predictable layout, clear headings, descriptive links, numbered steps, generous spacing, and simple words. Avoid relying on color alone. Keep line lengths comfortable. Support printing. Assume low computer literacy and avoid making patients feel blamed for security warnings, slow drives, or missing files.
